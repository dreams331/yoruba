## Introduction

The Yoruba people are often spoken of as a single ethnic group, but in reality, they are a wide civilisation made up of many **subgroups**, each with its own history, dialect, political system, and cultural expressions.

These subgroups share a common ancestry rooted in **Ile-Ife**, regarded as the spiritual cradle of Yoruba civilisation, and linked through traditions that often trace origin to **Oduduwa**.

Yet over centuries, migration, geography, trade, and warfare created distinct identities within the Yoruba world.

Understanding these subgroups reveals that Yoruba civilisation is not monolithic, but a **network of interconnected kingdoms and communities**.

## 1. Oyo Yoruba (Ọ̀yọ́)

The **Oyo Yoruba** are historically one of the most powerful subgroups, known for building the great **Oyo Empire**.

### Origins

They trace their lineage to early descendants of Odùduwà who settled in the savannah region north of Ile-Ife.

### Key Features

* Highly centralised political system
* Powerful cavalry-based military
* Imperial administration across Yorubaland

The ruler, the **Alaafin of Oyo**, became one of the most influential monarchs in West African history.

## 2. Ife Yoruba (Ilé-Ifẹ̀)

The Ife Yoruba are considered the **spiritual origin group** of all Yoruba subgroups.

### Origins

They are associated with the earliest settlement of Yoruba civilisation at **Ile-Ife**, where creation myths place the beginning of the world.

### Key Features

* Spiritual authority over Yoruba identity
* Advanced ancient art (bronze and terracotta sculptures)
* Religious and ritual significance

The ruler, the **Ooni of Ife**, is regarded as a custodian of Yoruba spirituality.

## 3. Ijebu Yoruba (Ìjẹ̀bú)

The **Ijebu Yoruba** are known for commerce, discipline, and strong economic systems.

### Origins

They are believed to have migrated from Ile-Ife and settled in forest-coastal trade corridors.

### Key Features

* Highly organised trade networks
* Strong regulatory systems on commerce routes
* Rich cultural festivals such as Ojude Oba

Their ruler is the **Awujale**, a highly respected traditional authority.

## 4. Egba Yoruba (Ẹ̀gbá)

The Egba Yoruba are historically associated with migration and settlement under pressure from war.

### Origins

Egba communities migrated and eventually settled around **Olumo Rock**, forming what became **Abeokuta**.

### Key Features

* Strong refugee-based origin story
* Unified under defensive settlement systems
* Strong urban development in Abeokuta

They are known for resilience and unity formed through survival.

## 5. Ekiti Yoruba (Èkìtì)

The Ekiti Yoruba are known for intelligence and independent hill settlements.

### Origins

They developed from early Yoruba migrations into forested highlands.

### Key Features

* Independent kingdoms rather than a single empire
* Strong intellectual and oral traditions
* Hill-based geography influencing settlement

Ekiti is widely associated with wisdom and learning within Yoruba culture.

## 6. Ijesha Yoruba (Ìjẹ̀ṣà)

The Ijesha Yoruba are closely tied to forest settlements and structured monarchy.

### Origins

They are linked to migration from Ile-Ife into eastern Yoruba forest regions.

### Key Features

* Strong monarchical system led by Owa Obokun
* Agricultural and forest-based economy
* Emphasis on wisdom and cultural preservation

The **Owa Obokun** remains central to their identity.

## 7. Ondo Yoruba

The Ondo Yoruba developed in the southern forest belt.

### Origins

They are linked to Yoruba migration traditions involving royal or noble settlers moving southward.

### Key Features

* Forest-based monarchy system
* Strong cultural identity
* Balanced diplomacy with neighbouring kingdoms

Their ruler is the **Osemawe**.

## 8. Ketu Yoruba (Kétu)

The Ketu Yoruba are found mainly in present-day Benin Republic.

### Origins

They are among the earliest Yoruba-related kingdoms outside Nigeria, maintaining strong ties to Ile-Ife traditions.

### Key Features

* Cross-border Yoruba identity
* Strong cultural preservation in Benin Republic
* Historical trade and political links

Ketu represents the early spread of Yoruba civilisation beyond modern Nigeria.

## Shared Identity Across Subgroups

Despite their differences, all Yoruba subgroups share:

* Common linguistic roots (Èdè Yorùbá)
* Spiritual systems (Ifá and Òrìṣà traditions)
* Historical connection to Ile-Ife
* Cultural values such as respect, wisdom, and community

This unity in diversity is one of the defining strengths of Yoruba civilisation.

## Conclusion

The Yoruba world is not a single uniform identity but a **civilisation of interconnected subgroups**, each shaped by geography, migration, and history.

From the powerful Oyo Empire to the intellectual Ekiti highlands, from the trading brilliance of Ijebu to the spiritual authority of Ile-Ife, each subgroup contributes a unique thread to the larger Yoruba tapestry.

Together, they form a civilisation that is both diverse and unified, ancient and evolving, local and global.

In understanding these subgroups, we see that Yoruba identity is not just inherited — it is continuously lived, reshaped, and celebrated across generations.
