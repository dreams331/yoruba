## Introduction

The story of the founding of **Akure** is one of the most treasured traditions in Yoruba history. As the capital of present-day **Ondo State**, Akure has grown into a major political, educational, and commercial centre. Yet its origins lie in ancient Yoruba traditions that connect the kingdom to **Ile-Ife**, the ancestral homeland of the Yoruba people.

Like many early Yoruba kingdoms, the history of Akure combines oral tradition, royal genealogies, and historical scholarship. While some details cannot be confirmed through contemporary written records, the traditions have been carefully preserved by generations of Akure people and remain central to the kingdom's identity.

## The Journey from Ile-Ife

According to Akure tradition, the kingdom was founded by **Asodeboyede**, also known as **Omoremilekun Asodeboyede**, a prince of Ile-Ife and a descendant of **Oduduwa**.

Like many Yoruba princes, Asodeboyede was said to have left Ile-Ife with members of his household, loyal followers, hunters, warriors, and advisers to establish a new kingdom.

His migration formed part of the wider movement through which many Yoruba kingdoms were founded across present-day south-western Nigeria.

## "Àkún Rẹ̀" – The Origin of the Name Akure

One of the best-known traditions explains the origin of the name **Akure** through an incident involving the founding prince.

As the story is told, while travelling through the forest, Asodeboyede accidentally broke the string of royal beads around his waist. When the beads were successfully gathered and restrung, those around him exclaimed:

**"Àkún rẹ̀!"**

This Yoruba expression can be interpreted as **"the beads have been gathered"** or **"your beads have been recovered."**

Over time, oral tradition holds that the phrase gradually evolved into the name **Akure**.

Although historians cannot verify the linguistic development with certainty, the story remains one of the kingdom's most cherished founding traditions and symbolises restoration, perseverance, and divine favour.

## Choosing the Settlement

After continuing their journey, Asodeboyede and his followers selected a favourable location for permanent settlement.

The site offered several advantages:

* Fertile farmland.
* Reliable water sources.
* Forest resources.
* Good hunting grounds.
* Natural protection.

These conditions enabled the new community to flourish and gradually develop into a recognised Yoruba kingdom.

## The Deji of Akure

The traditional ruler of Akure bears the title **Deji of Akure**.

The Deji serves as:

* Custodian of Akure's traditions.
* Symbol of the kingdom's unity.
* Guardian of its customs.
* Leader of important cultural ceremonies.

The institution of the Deji has endured for centuries and remains one of the most respected traditional monarchies in Yorubaland.

## Growth into a Prosperous Kingdom

Akure prospered because of its strategic location and productive environment.

Its economy developed through:

* Farming.
* Hunting.
* Local crafts.
* Ironworking.
* Regional trade.

As commerce expanded, Akure established strong relationships with neighbouring Yoruba kingdoms, including **Ado-Ekiti**, **Ilesa**, **Owo**, and **Benin City**.

These connections strengthened the kingdom's political and economic influence.

## Akure Through the Centuries

Like many Yoruba kingdoms, Akure experienced periods of conflict, diplomacy, and political change.

During the nineteenth century, the kingdom navigated the wider Yoruba wars while maintaining its own traditional institutions.

Under British colonial rule, Akure became an important administrative centre, and after Nigeria's independence it continued to grow rapidly.

Today, it serves as the capital of Ondo State and remains one of the fastest-growing cities in south-western Nigeria.

## Culture and Traditions

Akure continues to preserve many aspects of its cultural heritage.

These include:

* Traditional festivals.
* Royal ceremonies.
* Yoruba language.
* Indigenous crafts.
* Oral history.
* Respect for traditional institutions.

The annual celebration of the kingdom's history reinforces the connection between modern Akure and its ancestral origins.

## Historical and Cultural Significance

The founding story of Akure illustrates several important themes in Yoruba history:

* Migration from Ile-Ife.
* The establishment of new kingdoms.
* The importance of royal ancestry.
* The role of oral tradition in preserving history.
* The resilience of communities that adapted to new environments.

Although historians continue to study the early history of Akure, its founding traditions remain an essential part of the kingdom's cultural identity.

## Conclusion

The founding of **Akure** reflects the broader story of Yoruba migration, settlement, and state-building. According to long-standing oral tradition, the kingdom was established by **Asodeboyede**, a prince from **Ile-Ife**, whose journey led to the creation of one of Yorubaland's enduring kingdoms. The famous story of the recovered royal beads, from which the name Akure is traditionally derived, continues to symbolise hope, perseverance, and divine guidance.

Today, Akure stands as a thriving modern city while remaining deeply connected to its ancient roots. Its history, preserved through oral tradition, royal institutions, and cultural celebrations, reminds us that the strength of a kingdom lies not only in its age but also in its ability to preserve its heritage while embracing the future.
