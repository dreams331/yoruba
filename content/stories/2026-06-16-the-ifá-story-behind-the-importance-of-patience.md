## Introduction

Among the many virtues celebrated in Yoruba philosophy, few are praised as highly as **sùúrù**, meaning patience. In a world that often rewards haste, immediate gratification, and quick results, Ifá repeatedly reminds humanity that some blessings arrive only through endurance, discipline, and trust in divine timing.

The Yoruba say:

> **"Sùúrù l'ẹ̀rè."**

Meaning:

> **"Patience is profitable."**

This proverb is not simply advice for daily living. It reflects one of the central teachings of Ifá. Again and again, the sacred verses warn against impulsiveness and teach that what is pursued without patience often leads to regret.

One of the most profound stories illustrating this truth is found within the wisdom of Ifá and the experiences of **Òrúnmìlà**, the custodian of divine knowledge.

## The Day Impatience Tested Humanity

According to Ifá tradition, there once lived a man who desperately wanted success.

He wanted wealth.

He wanted recognition.

He wanted respect from his community.

Most of all, he wanted these things immediately.

He visited several people seeking advice, but every instruction that required effort, sacrifice, or waiting frustrated him. He believed that intelligence alone should guarantee quick rewards.

Finally, he was advised to consult Òrúnmìlà.

The wise sage performed divination and revealed that the man possessed the destiny to prosper. However, Ifá warned that his blessings would unfold gradually.

Òrúnmìlà instructed him to do three things.

First, he was to perform the recommended **ẹbọ**.

Second, he was to continue working diligently.

Third, he was to exercise patience.

The man accepted the first two instructions.

The third one troubled him.

## The Temptation of Immediate Results

Days passed.

Then weeks.

The success he expected did not appear.

He became angry.

"Perhaps the divination was wrong," he thought.

"Perhaps there is a quicker path."

Ignoring the wisdom of Ifá, he abandoned the disciplined work he had begun. Instead, he pursued shortcuts and opportunities that promised instant rewards.

At first, these alternatives appeared attractive.

They required little effort.

They seemed to produce immediate gains.

The man congratulated himself for rejecting what he considered slow wisdom.

Then everything changed.

The ventures collapsed.

Promises made to him were broken.

People he trusted disappeared.

The little he had gained vanished.

He returned to Òrúnmìlà with disappointment and shame.

## Òrúnmìlà's Lesson

When the man confessed what had happened, Òrúnmìlà did not ridicule him.

Instead, he asked a simple question.

> "Can a farmer harvest yam the day after planting it?"

The man lowered his head.

Òrúnmìlà continued:

> "The seed buried beneath the earth is not idle. Though unseen, it is growing. The person who digs it up every day to check its progress destroys the harvest they desire."

The man understood.

Not all silence means failure.

Not all waiting means abandonment.

Some processes require time.

The wisdom of Ifá teaches that destiny unfolds according to seasons.

## Patience as an Expression of Wisdom

In Yoruba thought, patience is not weakness.

It is strength under control.

A patient person is not passive.

They continue to act, prepare, and persevere while recognising that not everything can be forced.

Ifá teaches that patience involves:

* Remaining steadfast during difficulty,
* Trusting the process without becoming complacent,
* Resisting the temptation of destructive shortcuts,
* Maintaining integrity when rewards seem delayed.

Patience is therefore closely connected to wisdom.

It allows people to make thoughtful decisions rather than emotional reactions.

## The Story of the Farmer and the Palm Tree

Another Yoruba teaching compares patience to the cultivation of a palm tree.

A person who plants a palm kernel does not expect fruit immediately.

The tree grows slowly.

Its roots strengthen beneath the soil.

Its trunk rises gradually.

Years later, it provides oil, shade, and sustenance for generations.

The Yoruba proverb says:

> **"Bí a bá ní sùúrù, a máa jẹ ẹ̀so àgbàdo."**

Meaning:

> **"If we are patient, we shall enjoy the harvest."**

The proverb reminds people that enduring rewards often require enduring effort.

## Why Ifá Values Patience

Within the verses of Ifá, impatience is frequently associated with misfortune.

People who refuse to wait often:

* Ignore wise counsel,
* Make reckless decisions,
* Abandon worthwhile pursuits,
* Damage valuable relationships,
* Exchange lasting blessings for temporary satisfaction.

Patience, by contrast, creates room for discernment.

It gives people time to:

* Think clearly,
* Observe carefully,
* Learn from experience,
* Align themselves with their destiny.

Through patience, wisdom matures.

## Patience and Destiny

One of the most important teachings of Ifá is that every person comes into the world with an **Orí**, a chosen destiny.

However, destiny is not fulfilled in an instant.

A gifted child must still learn.

A future leader must still develop character.

A talented artist must still practise.

A farmer must still cultivate the land.

Ifá teaches that blessings often arrive through preparation.

Patience enables people to grow into the destinies they have received.

Without patience, they may abandon their path before its fruits appear.

## Patience in Relationships and Community

The value of sùúrù extends beyond personal ambition.

It shapes family life and community relationships.

Patience allows people to:

* Listen before reacting,
* Forgive mistakes,
* Resolve conflicts peacefully,
* Teach children with understanding,
* Build trust over time.

Communities thrive when members exercise restraint and empathy rather than impulsiveness.

The Yoruba recognise that harmony cannot flourish where everyone insists on immediate gratification.

## Patience Does Not Mean Inaction

A common misunderstanding is that patience requires doing nothing.

Ifá rejects this idea.

True patience involves active participation.

It means:

* Working while waiting,
* Preparing while hoping,
* Remaining disciplined despite uncertainty.

The patient person plants the seed, waters it faithfully, removes weeds, and trusts that harvest will come in its proper season.

Patience without effort becomes laziness.

Effort without patience becomes frustration.

Wisdom requires both.

## The Enduring Message of Ifá

The story of the impatient man and Òrúnmìlà continues to resonate because it reflects a universal human struggle.

Everyone has experienced moments of wanting immediate answers, instant success, or quick solutions.

Ifá gently reminds us that life rarely unfolds according to our preferred timetable.

Some lessons require time.

Some blessings require preparation.

Some destinies require growth.

The waiting period is not always empty.

Often, it is the very place where character is formed.

## Conclusion

The Ifá story behind the importance of patience teaches that **sùúrù is not merely the ability to wait. It is the wisdom to wait well.**

Through the guidance of Òrúnmìlà, the Yoruba learned that lasting success cannot always be rushed, and that shortcuts often lead people away from the blessings meant for them.

Patience strengthens character.

Patience protects judgment.

Patience allows destiny to unfold in its proper season.

As the Yoruba wisely say:

> **"Sùúrù l'ẹ̀rè."**
>
> **"Patience brings its reward."**

In a hurried world that constantly urges people to seek immediate results, the wisdom of Ifá offers a timeless reminder. Trust the process, continue the work before you, cultivate good character, and allow life to ripen as it should. The sweetest harvests are often those that require the greatest patience.
