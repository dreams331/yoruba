## Ìwòrì-Ọ̀kànràn

### Overview

Sharp truth cuts through confusion. Honest vision settles conflict, even if the process is uncomfortable.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Ọ̀kànràn Méjì** — conflict, truth-telling, sharp consequences (Ọya / Ògún)


### Guidance

Speak plainly and with insight, and accept that clarity can sting before it heals.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double) and the left column is Ọ̀kànràn Méjì (double · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìwòrì-Ọ̀kànràn means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### From Ọ̀kànràn Méjì

Ọ̀kànràn exposes what is hidden and warns against betrayal, gossip, and dishonesty. It teaches that confronting conflict honestly, though painful, prevents greater harm later.

*Òtítọ́ máa ń dùn, ṣùgbọ́n irọ́ máa ń pa ni níkẹyìn.*  
"Truth may sting, but a lie eventually destroys."

### Putting the two together

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception. At the same time, in everyday terms, this figure advises resolving disputes directly rather than letting them grow in silence. It also asks a person to think before speaking, because words once said cannot be taken back.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
