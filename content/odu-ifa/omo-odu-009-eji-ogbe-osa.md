## Ogbè-Ọ̀sá

### Overview

A bright path crosses a whirlwind. Sudden change threatens plans, yet the figure promises protection to those who stay grounded.

### Governing Influences

* **Èjì Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)
* **Ọ̀sá Méjì** — upheaval, the whirlwind, sudden change, protection from harm (Ọya)


### Guidance

Expect disruption, keep your counsel, and hold on to your principles while things settle.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single) and the left column is Ọ̀sá Méjì (double · single · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ogbè-Ọ̀sá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### From Ọ̀sá Méjì

Ọ̀sá teaches resilience through sudden disruption. What feels destructive can also clear away what no longer serves growth. It calls for protection (ẹbọ) and steady faith during turbulent seasons.

*Afẹ́fẹ́ tó le kì í fi ìgi sílẹ̀ láìṣí àyípadà.*  
"A strong wind never leaves a tree unchanged."

### Putting the two together

When this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through. At the same time, practically, this figure advises preparation and calm. Keep essentials secure, avoid reacting to every disturbance, and look for what the change makes possible once it settles.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
