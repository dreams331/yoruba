## Òtúrá-Ìká

### Overview

Seeing through bitterness. Understanding the real cause of someone's anger helps you respond well.

### Governing Influences

- **Òtúrá Méjì** — transformation, clarity after confusion, truth revealed (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọbàtálá)
- **Ìká Méjì** — cruelty, resentment, the need for restraint (Ọya / Èṣù)


### Guidance

Try to understand the person before answering the insult.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Òtúrá Méjì (single · double · single · single) and the left column is Ìká Méjì (double · single · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Òtúrá-Ìká means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Òtúrá Méjì

Òtúrá teaches that confusion and difficulty are often temporary, giving way to clarity for those who remain patient and truthful. It marks turning points and the wisdom gained through transformation.

*Kùràkùrà ọjọ́ ni ìmọ́lẹ̀ ọjọ́ tuntun.*  
"The dimness before dawn gives way to the light of a new day."

### From Ìká Méjì

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

*Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá.*  
"A cruel heart never brings peace."

### Putting the two together

In practice, the figure encourages a person not to make final decisions in the middle of confusion. Wait for understanding, speak calmly, and expect that the picture will clear for those who stay honest and patient. At the same time, this figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
