## Ògúndá-Ọ̀ṣẹ́

### Overview

Work bears sweet fruit. The figure foretells rewards for those who persevere and encourages gratitude and generosity.

### Governing Influences

- **Ògúndá Méjì** — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)
- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)


### Guidance

Share the proceeds of your labour with those who helped.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double) and the left column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ògúndá-Ọ̀ṣẹ́ means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

*Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n.*  
"A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose. At the same time, practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
