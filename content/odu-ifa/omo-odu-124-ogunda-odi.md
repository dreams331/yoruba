## Ògúndá-Òdí

### Overview

Quiet work builds a hidden foundation. Effort that nobody sees is shaping something that will later be solid.

### Governing Influences

- **Ògúndá Méjì** — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)
- **Òdí Méjì** — the womb, mystery, transformation, hidden depth (Ọya / Yemọja)


### Guidance

Keep building patiently, even if no one notices yet.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double) and the left column is Òdí Méjì (single · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ògúndá-Òdí means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

*Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n.*  
"A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

### From Òdí Méjì

Òdí teaches patience with processes that are not yet visible — pregnancy, growth, and transformation take time in darkness before they emerge into light. It also warns against forcing what must unfold naturally.

*Ohun tó wà nínú, kì í fi ara rẹ̀ hàn ní ọ̀nà kan ṣoṣo.*  
"What is within does not reveal itself in only one way."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose. At the same time, in practice, this figure advises a person not to uncover a plan, a project or a relationship too early. Give it time and shelter. It also warns against forcing outcomes, because pressure applied at the wrong stage can damage what is forming.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
