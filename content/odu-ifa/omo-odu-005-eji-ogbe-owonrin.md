## Ogbè-Ọ̀wọ́nrín

### Overview

Fortune is bright but unstable. The caution is that good luck can turn quickly, and humility is what keeps a blessing in place.

### Governing Influences

* **Èjì Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)
* **Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì** — instability, change, the trickster's lesson, humility (Èṣù / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Stay modest in success and prepare for changing circumstances.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single) and the left column is Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì (double · double · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ogbè-Ọ̀wọ́nrín means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### From Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì

Ọ̀wọ́nrín teaches humility in success and resilience in reversal. It reminds us that fortunes change, so one must remain adaptable, avoid arrogance, and treat others well on the way up.

*Ohun tó dúró jẹ́ẹ́, ni yíò yí padà.*  
"What stands firm today may be overturned tomorrow."

### Putting the two together

When this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through. At the same time, this figure suggests that a position held today can change tomorrow, so kindness and good relationships are better security than status. It also advises preparing for uncertainty rather than pretending it will not come.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
