## Ògúndá-Ìká

### Overview

Hard effort invites envy. Visible progress attracts resentment, and quiet persistence is the best response.

### Governing Influences

- **Ògúndá Méjì** — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)
- **Ìká Méjì** — cruelty, resentment, the need for restraint (Ọya / Èṣù)


### Guidance

Do not boast of your gains, and keep a steady pace.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double) and the left column is Ìká Méjì (double · single · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ògúndá-Ìká means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

*Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n.*  
"A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

### From Ìká Méjì

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

*Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá.*  
"A cruel heart never brings peace."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose. At the same time, this figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
