## Òfún-Ìwòrì

### Overview

Integrity grounded in self-knowledge. A person who knows their own heart is hard to corrupt.

### Governing Influences

- **Òfún Méjì** — purity, old age, completion, moral integrity (Ọbàtálá / Olódùmarè)
- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Examine your motives regularly.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Òfún Méjì (double · single · double · single) and the left column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Òfún-Ìwòrì means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Òfún Méjì

Òfún, closely tied to Ọbàtálá, teaches purity of character, patience, and the moral discipline required to age gracefully and finish life's journey with integrity intact.

*Funfun ni àrà; kò gba ìdọ̀tí láti dúró funfun.*  
"White is sacred; it takes discipline to remain unstained."

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### Putting the two together

In daily terms, this figure advises keeping one's word, being slow to anger, and finishing what one starts. It is also a reminder that integrity is maintained through small choices made consistently. At the same time, tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
