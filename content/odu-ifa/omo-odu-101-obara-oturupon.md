## Ọ̀bàrà-Òtúrúpọ̀n

### Overview

Responsibility includes health. It ties leadership to caring for the body and for people under your care.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)
- **Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì** — illness and healing, secrets of the body, patience (Ọbàtálá / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Look after your own wellbeing and that of your dependants.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double) and the left column is Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì (double · double · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀bàrà-Òtúrúpọ̀n means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### From Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì

Òtúrúpọ̀n calls for patience, proper diagnosis before action, and respect for the body's natural healing processes. It also symbolises the wisdom that comes from surviving hardship.

*Àrùn tí a kò tọ́jú lọ́nà tí ó tọ́ kì í yá.*  
"An illness not treated in the right way does not heal."

### Putting the two together

Read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements. At the same time, read in daily life, this figure advises seeking the right help early, following treatment through, and resting enough. It also encourages care for people who are unwell, since recovery is helped by support.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
