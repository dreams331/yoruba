## Òtúrá-Ọ̀bàrà

### Overview

A new understanding tests your pride. The truth you have learned may mean admitting you were wrong.

### Governing Influences

- **Òtúrá Méjì** — transformation, clarity after confusion, truth revealed (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọbàtálá)
- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)


### Guidance

Admit the mistake and move forward.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Òtúrá Méjì (single · double · single · single) and the left column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Òtúrá-Ọ̀bàrà means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Òtúrá Méjì

Òtúrá teaches that confusion and difficulty are often temporary, giving way to clarity for those who remain patient and truthful. It marks turning points and the wisdom gained through transformation.

*Kùràkùrà ọjọ́ ni ìmọ́lẹ̀ ọjọ́ tuntun.*  
"The dimness before dawn gives way to the light of a new day."

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### Putting the two together

In practice, the figure encourages a person not to make final decisions in the middle of confusion. Wait for understanding, speak calmly, and expect that the picture will clear for those who stay honest and patient. At the same time, read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
