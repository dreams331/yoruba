## Ìrosùn-Òtúrá

### Overview

Clarity follows a cautionary lesson. After a period of trouble, the picture clears and the right course is revealed.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìrosùn Méjì** — blood, sacrifice, warning, consequence (Ṣàngó / Ọya)
- **Òtúrá Méjì** — transformation, clarity after confusion, truth revealed (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọbàtálá)


### Guidance

Learn from what has happened and move forward with a clear mind.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìrosùn Méjì (single · single · double · double) and the left column is Òtúrá Méjì (single · double · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìrosùn-Òtúrá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìrosùn Méjì

Ìrosùn warns that actions have irreversible consequences, and that ẹbọ (sacrifice) — whether of time, pride, or resources — is often required to avert disaster. It teaches accountability.

*Ẹ̀jẹ̀ tí a tú sílẹ̀ kì í padà sí inú ara.*  
"Blood that is spilled does not return to the body."

### From Òtúrá Méjì

Òtúrá teaches that confusion and difficulty are often temporary, giving way to clarity for those who remain patient and truthful. It marks turning points and the wisdom gained through transformation.

*Kùràkùrà ọjọ́ ni ìmọ́lẹ̀ ọjọ́ tuntun.*  
"The dimness before dawn gives way to the light of a new day."

### Putting the two together

Tradition reads this figure as a call to slow down and take stock before acting, especially when emotions run high. Where harm has been done, it points toward repair: acknowledging it, making amends, and not repeating it. At the same time, in practice, the figure encourages a person not to make final decisions in the middle of confusion. Wait for understanding, speak calmly, and expect that the picture will clear for those who stay honest and patient.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
