## Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀ṣẹ́

### Overview

Honour is sweetest when shared. Blessings grow when a leader is generous and grateful.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)
- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)


### Guidance

Share credit and rewards with your community.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double) and the left column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀ṣẹ́ means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### Putting the two together

Read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements. At the same time, practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
