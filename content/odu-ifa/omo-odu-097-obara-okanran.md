## Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀kànràn

### Overview

Pride meets conflict. The caution is that arrogance produces quarrels, and honest, measured speech prevents them.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)
- **Ọ̀kànràn Méjì** — conflict, truth-telling, sharp consequences (Ọya / Ògún)


### Guidance

Choose words carefully, especially when you hold authority.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double) and the left column is Ọ̀kànràn Méjì (double · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀kànràn means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### From Ọ̀kànràn Méjì

Ọ̀kànràn exposes what is hidden and warns against betrayal, gossip, and dishonesty. It teaches that confronting conflict honestly, though painful, prevents greater harm later.

*Òtítọ́ máa ń dùn, ṣùgbọ́n irọ́ máa ń pa ni níkẹyìn.*  
"Truth may sting, but a lie eventually destroys."

### Putting the two together

Read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements. At the same time, in everyday terms, this figure advises resolving disputes directly rather than letting them grow in silence. It also asks a person to think before speaking, because words once said cannot be taken back.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
