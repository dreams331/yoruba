## Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Òtúrúpọ̀n

### Overview

Health as a blessing. Good health is wealth, and it should not be taken for granted.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)
- **Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì** — illness and healing, secrets of the body, patience (Ọbàtálá / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Look after your body as you would your savings.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double) and the left column is Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì (double · double · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Òtúrúpọ̀n means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### From Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì

Òtúrúpọ̀n calls for patience, proper diagnosis before action, and respect for the body's natural healing processes. It also symbolises the wisdom that comes from surviving hardship.

*Àrùn tí a kò tọ́jú lọ́nà tí ó tọ́ kì í yá.*  
"An illness not treated in the right way does not heal."

### Putting the two together

Practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline. At the same time, read in daily life, this figure advises seeking the right help early, following treatment through, and resting enough. It also encourages care for people who are unwell, since recovery is helped by support.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
