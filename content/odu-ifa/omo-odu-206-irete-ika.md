## Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ìká

### Overview

Persistence against resentment. Others may wish you to fail, and quiet steady progress is the best answer.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì** — perseverance despite setbacks, hidden victory (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọya)
- **Ìká Méjì** — cruelty, resentment, the need for restraint (Ọya / Èṣù)


### Guidance

Let your results answer your critics.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì (single · single · double · single) and the left column is Ìká Méjì (double · single · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ìká means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì

Ìrẹtẹ̀ teaches resilience in adversity, especially struggles others cannot see. It reassures that persistence — even quiet, unglamorous effort — leads to eventual triumph.

*Ìṣẹ́gun tí a kò fojú rí ni í dùn jù.*  
"The victory unseen by others is often the sweetest."

### From Ìká Méjì

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

*Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá.*  
"A cruel heart never brings peace."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as reassurance for those who feel stuck. Keep going, keep records of progress, and avoid measuring yourself against people whose circumstances differ. Results often arrive later than hoped but are lasting when earned. At the same time, this figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
