## Òdí-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú

### Overview

A hidden process meets the ancestral realm. It points to inherited patterns, transformation and the quiet work of renewal.

### Governing Influences

- **Òdí Méjì** — the womb, mystery, transformation, hidden depth (Ọya / Yemọja)
- **Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì** — endings, the ancestors, the unseen world, mortality (Ikú (Death) / Ọ̀sun)


### Guidance

Honour your lineage and allow necessary changes to unfold slowly.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Òdí Méjì (single · double · double · single) and the left column is Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì (double · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Òdí-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Òdí Méjì

Òdí teaches patience with processes that are not yet visible — pregnancy, growth, and transformation take time in darkness before they emerge into light. It also warns against forcing what must unfold naturally.

*Ohun tó wà nínú, kì í fi ara rẹ̀ hàn ní ọ̀nà kan ṣoṣo.*  
"What is within does not reveal itself in only one way."

### From Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú teaches respect for the ancestors, acceptance of mortality, and the understanding that every ending clears space for renewal. It warns against arrogance in the face of life's fragility.

*Òkú tó sun, kì í jí mọ́; ẹ̀mí tó lọ, kì í padà bí ara.*  
"The dead that sleeps does not wake again; the spirit that departs does not return in the same body."

### Putting the two together

In practice, this figure advises a person not to uncover a plan, a project or a relationship too early. Give it time and shelter. It also warns against forcing outcomes, because pressure applied at the wrong stage can damage what is forming. At the same time, read in daily life, this figure asks a person to honour those who came before, settle unfinished obligations, and stop treating limits as enemies. It cautions against pride about health, wealth or status, since all of them are borrowed for a time.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
