## Ìká-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú

### Overview

Resentment follows the family. Take care here: old grievances passed down through generations should be settled with the help of elders.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìká Méjì** — cruelty, resentment, the need for restraint (Ọya / Èṣù)
- **Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì** — endings, the ancestors, the unseen world, mortality (Ikú (Death) / Ọ̀sun)


### Guidance

Make peace with the past so it does not shape the future.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìká Méjì (double · single · double · double) and the left column is Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì (double · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìká-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìká Méjì

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

*Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá.*  
"A cruel heart never brings peace."

### From Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú teaches respect for the ancestors, acceptance of mortality, and the understanding that every ending clears space for renewal. It warns against arrogance in the face of life's fragility.

*Òkú tó sun, kì í jí mọ́; ẹ̀mí tó lọ, kì í padà bí ara.*  
"The dead that sleeps does not wake again; the spirit that departs does not return in the same body."

### Putting the two together

This figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment. At the same time, read in daily life, this figure asks a person to honour those who came before, settle unfinished obligations, and stop treating limits as enemies. It cautions against pride about health, wealth or status, since all of them are borrowed for a time.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
