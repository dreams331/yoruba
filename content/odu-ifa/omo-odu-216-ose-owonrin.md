## Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ọ̀wọ́nrín

### Overview

Good fortune in a changing market. Prosperity may move from one place to another, so hold your gains loosely.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)
- **Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì** — instability, change, the trickster's lesson, humility (Èṣù / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Do not put all your trust in one source of income.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double) and the left column is Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì (double · double · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ọ̀wọ́nrín means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### From Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì

Ọ̀wọ́nrín teaches humility in success and resilience in reversal. It reminds us that fortunes change, so one must remain adaptable, avoid arrogance, and treat others well on the way up.

*Ohun tó dúró jẹ́ẹ́, ni yíò yí padà.*  
"What stands firm today may be overturned tomorrow."

### Putting the two together

Practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline. At the same time, this figure suggests that a position held today can change tomorrow, so kindness and good relationships are better security than status. It also advises preparing for uncertainty rather than pretending it will not come.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
