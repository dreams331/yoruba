## Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú

### Overview

A blessing rooted in the family. Prosperity is tied to the memory of those who prepared the ground.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)
- **Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì** — endings, the ancestors, the unseen world, mortality (Ikú (Death) / Ọ̀sun)


### Guidance

Honour your elders as you enjoy the benefits of their work.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double) and the left column is Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì (double · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ọ̀yẹ̀kú means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### From Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú teaches respect for the ancestors, acceptance of mortality, and the understanding that every ending clears space for renewal. It warns against arrogance in the face of life's fragility.

*Òkú tó sun, kì í jí mọ́; ẹ̀mí tó lọ, kì í padà bí ara.*  
"The dead that sleeps does not wake again; the spirit that departs does not return in the same body."

### Putting the two together

Practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline. At the same time, read in daily life, this figure asks a person to honour those who came before, settle unfinished obligations, and stop treating limits as enemies. It cautions against pride about health, wealth or status, since all of them are borrowed for a time.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
