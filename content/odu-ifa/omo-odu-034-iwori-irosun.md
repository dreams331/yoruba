## Ìwòrì-Ìrosùn

### Overview

Perception is challenged by consequence. The caution is that ignoring the signs leads to a costly lesson, and sacrifice can restore order.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Ìrosùn Méjì** — blood, sacrifice, warning, consequence (Ṣàngó / Ọya)


### Guidance

Pay attention to warnings, and act early to avoid avoidable losses.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double) and the left column is Ìrosùn Méjì (single · single · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìwòrì-Ìrosùn means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### From Ìrosùn Méjì

Ìrosùn warns that actions have irreversible consequences, and that ẹbọ (sacrifice) — whether of time, pride, or resources — is often required to avert disaster. It teaches accountability.

*Ẹ̀jẹ̀ tí a tú sílẹ̀ kì í padà sí inú ara.*  
"Blood that is spilled does not return to the body."

### Putting the two together

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception. At the same time, tradition reads this figure as a call to slow down and take stock before acting, especially when emotions run high. Where harm has been done, it points toward repair: acknowledging it, making amends, and not repeating it.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
