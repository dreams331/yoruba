## Ogbè-Ìrosùn

### Overview

Divine favour is tied to sacrifice. Blessings are available, but they follow a willingness to give something up and to keep promises made to the Òrìṣà.

### Governing Influences

* **Èjì Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)
* **Ìrosùn Méjì** — blood, sacrifice, warning, consequence (Ṣàngó / Ọya)


### Guidance

Make the offering or commitment you have been postponing, and keep your word.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single) and the left column is Ìrosùn Méjì (single · single · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ogbè-Ìrosùn means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### From Ìrosùn Méjì

Ìrosùn warns that actions have irreversible consequences, and that ẹbọ (sacrifice) — whether of time, pride, or resources — is often required to avert disaster. It teaches accountability.

*Ẹ̀jẹ̀ tí a tú sílẹ̀ kì í padà sí inú ara.*  
"Blood that is spilled does not return to the body."

### Putting the two together

When this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through. At the same time, tradition reads this figure as a call to slow down and take stock before acting, especially when emotions run high. Where harm has been done, it points toward repair: acknowledging it, making amends, and not repeating it.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
