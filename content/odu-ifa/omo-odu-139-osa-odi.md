## Ọ̀sá-Òdí

### Overview

Change is working in the dark. Transformation is under way but not yet visible, and the figure advises patience through the disruption.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀sá Méjì** — upheaval, the whirlwind, sudden change, protection from harm (Ọya)
- **Òdí Méjì** — the womb, mystery, transformation, hidden depth (Ọya / Yemọja)


### Guidance

Trust the process, and hold back from forcing outcomes.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀sá Méjì (double · single · single · single) and the left column is Òdí Méjì (single · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀sá-Òdí means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀sá Méjì

Ọ̀sá teaches resilience through sudden disruption. What feels destructive can also clear away what no longer serves growth. It calls for protection (ẹbọ) and steady faith during turbulent seasons.

*Afẹ́fẹ́ tó le kì í fi ìgi sílẹ̀ láìṣí àyípadà.*  
"A strong wind never leaves a tree unchanged."

### From Òdí Méjì

Òdí teaches patience with processes that are not yet visible — pregnancy, growth, and transformation take time in darkness before they emerge into light. It also warns against forcing what must unfold naturally.

*Ohun tó wà nínú, kì í fi ara rẹ̀ hàn ní ọ̀nà kan ṣoṣo.*  
"What is within does not reveal itself in only one way."

### Putting the two together

Practically, this figure advises preparation and calm. Keep essentials secure, avoid reacting to every disturbance, and look for what the change makes possible once it settles. At the same time, in practice, this figure advises a person not to uncover a plan, a project or a relationship too early. Give it time and shelter. It also warns against forcing outcomes, because pressure applied at the wrong stage can damage what is forming.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
