## Ìrosùn-Òdí

### Overview

A costly secret demands attention. Hidden matters carry consequences, and bringing them into the open prevents greater harm.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìrosùn Méjì** — blood, sacrifice, warning, consequence (Ṣàngó / Ọya)
- **Òdí Méjì** — the womb, mystery, transformation, hidden depth (Ọya / Yemọja)


### Guidance

Be truthful about what has been concealed and put things right.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìrosùn Méjì (single · single · double · double) and the left column is Òdí Méjì (single · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìrosùn-Òdí means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìrosùn Méjì

Ìrosùn warns that actions have irreversible consequences, and that ẹbọ (sacrifice) — whether of time, pride, or resources — is often required to avert disaster. It teaches accountability.

*Ẹ̀jẹ̀ tí a tú sílẹ̀ kì í padà sí inú ara.*  
"Blood that is spilled does not return to the body."

### From Òdí Méjì

Òdí teaches patience with processes that are not yet visible — pregnancy, growth, and transformation take time in darkness before they emerge into light. It also warns against forcing what must unfold naturally.

*Ohun tó wà nínú, kì í fi ara rẹ̀ hàn ní ọ̀nà kan ṣoṣo.*  
"What is within does not reveal itself in only one way."

### Putting the two together

Tradition reads this figure as a call to slow down and take stock before acting, especially when emotions run high. Where harm has been done, it points toward repair: acknowledging it, making amends, and not repeating it. At the same time, in practice, this figure advises a person not to uncover a plan, a project or a relationship too early. Give it time and shelter. It also warns against forcing outcomes, because pressure applied at the wrong stage can damage what is forming.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
