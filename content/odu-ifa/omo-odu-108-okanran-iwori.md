## Ọ̀kànràn-Ìwòrì

### Overview

Honest speech joins self-awareness. You can say hard things well only after you have looked honestly at yourself.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀kànràn Méjì** — conflict, truth-telling, sharp consequences (Ọya / Ògún)
- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Check your own part in a quarrel before you speak.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀kànràn Méjì (double · double · double · single) and the left column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀kànràn-Ìwòrì means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀kànràn Méjì

Ọ̀kànràn exposes what is hidden and warns against betrayal, gossip, and dishonesty. It teaches that confronting conflict honestly, though painful, prevents greater harm later.

*Òtítọ́ máa ń dùn, ṣùgbọ́n irọ́ máa ń pa ni níkẹyìn.*  
"Truth may sting, but a lie eventually destroys."

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### Putting the two together

In everyday terms, this figure advises resolving disputes directly rather than letting them grow in silence. It also asks a person to think before speaking, because words once said cannot be taken back. At the same time, tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
