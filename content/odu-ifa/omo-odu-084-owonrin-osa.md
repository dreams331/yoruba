## Ọ̀wọ́nrín-Ọ̀sá

### Overview

Double turbulence demands calm. It advises staying centred when events spin quickly and seeking shelter in sound principles.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì** — instability, change, the trickster's lesson, humility (Èṣù / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Ọ̀sá Méjì** — upheaval, the whirlwind, sudden change, protection from harm (Ọya)


### Guidance

Keep your composure and avoid major decisions in the middle of the storm.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì (double · double · single · single) and the left column is Ọ̀sá Méjì (double · single · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀wọ́nrín-Ọ̀sá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì

Ọ̀wọ́nrín teaches humility in success and resilience in reversal. It reminds us that fortunes change, so one must remain adaptable, avoid arrogance, and treat others well on the way up.

*Ohun tó dúró jẹ́ẹ́, ni yíò yí padà.*  
"What stands firm today may be overturned tomorrow."

### From Ọ̀sá Méjì

Ọ̀sá teaches resilience through sudden disruption. What feels destructive can also clear away what no longer serves growth. It calls for protection (ẹbọ) and steady faith during turbulent seasons.

*Afẹ́fẹ́ tó le kì í fi ìgi sílẹ̀ láìṣí àyípadà.*  
"A strong wind never leaves a tree unchanged."

### Putting the two together

This figure suggests that a position held today can change tomorrow, so kindness and good relationships are better security than status. It also advises preparing for uncertainty rather than pretending it will not come. At the same time, practically, this figure advises preparation and calm. Keep essentials secure, avoid reacting to every disturbance, and look for what the change makes possible once it settles.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
