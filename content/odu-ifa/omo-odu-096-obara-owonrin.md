## Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀wọ́nrín

### Overview

Power is unstable when pride grows. Status changes quickly for those who lose humility.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)
- **Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì** — instability, change, the trickster's lesson, humility (Èṣù / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Stay grounded and prepare for shifts in fortune.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double) and the left column is Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì (double · double · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀bàrà-Ọ̀wọ́nrín means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### From Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì

Ọ̀wọ́nrín teaches humility in success and resilience in reversal. It reminds us that fortunes change, so one must remain adaptable, avoid arrogance, and treat others well on the way up.

*Ohun tó dúró jẹ́ẹ́, ni yíò yí padà.*  
"What stands firm today may be overturned tomorrow."

### Putting the two together

Read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements. At the same time, this figure suggests that a position held today can change tomorrow, so kindness and good relationships are better security than status. It also advises preparing for uncertainty rather than pretending it will not come.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
