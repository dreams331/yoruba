## Ìwòrì-Òdí

### Overview

Hidden things come to the surface slowly. It speaks of mysteries, secrets and truths that mature in the dark before being revealed.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Òdí Méjì** — the womb, mystery, transformation, hidden depth (Ọya / Yemọja)


### Guidance

Be patient with what is still unclear and avoid forcing answers.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double) and the left column is Òdí Méjì (single · double · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìwòrì-Òdí means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### From Òdí Méjì

Òdí teaches patience with processes that are not yet visible — pregnancy, growth, and transformation take time in darkness before they emerge into light. It also warns against forcing what must unfold naturally.

*Ohun tó wà nínú, kì í fi ara rẹ̀ hàn ní ọ̀nà kan ṣoṣo.*  
"What is within does not reveal itself in only one way."

### Putting the two together

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception. At the same time, in practice, this figure advises a person not to uncover a plan, a project or a relationship too early. Give it time and shelter. It also warns against forcing outcomes, because pressure applied at the wrong stage can damage what is forming.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
