## Ogbè-Ọ̀bàrà

### Overview

Natural leadership is tested by pride. It grants honour and standing, then asks whether you can carry them without boasting.

### Governing Influences

* **Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)
* **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)


### Guidance

Lead by example, speak less of your achievements, and let your work speak for you.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single) and the left column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ogbè-Ọ̀bàrà means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### Putting the two together

When this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through. At the same time, read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
