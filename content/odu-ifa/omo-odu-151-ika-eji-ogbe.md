## Ìká-ÈjìOgbè

### Overview

A hard heart is exposed to the light. Cruelty and bitterness are revealed for what they are, and offers a chance to start over.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìká Méjì** — cruelty, resentment, the need for restraint (Ọya / Èṣù)
- **Èjì Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)


### Guidance

Examine your own bitterness and choose a better beginning.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìká Méjì (double · single · double · double) and the left column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìká-ÈjìOgbè means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìká Méjì

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

*Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá.*  
"A cruel heart never brings peace."

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### Putting the two together

This figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment. At the same time, when this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
