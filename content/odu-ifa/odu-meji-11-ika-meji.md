## Ìká Méjì

> *"Ìkà ọkàn kì í mú àlàáfíà wá."*
> *"A cruel heart never brings peace."*

---

### Meaning

The Odu that warns against cruelty, resentment, and the corrosive effects of malice.

### Associated Òrìṣà

Ọya / Èṣù

### Life Lesson

Ìká warns of the dangers of bitterness, envy, and vengeance — both suffering it and inflicting it. It teaches restraint, forgiveness, and guarding one's heart against becoming hardened by pain.

### Overview

Ìká Méjì is concerned with the inner life of bitterness, envy and cruelty, both in oneself and in others. It is a moral figure. Its attention is on how unresolved pain can harden into harmful behaviour, and on the self-control needed to prevent that.

### Core Themes

- Restraint and self-mastery
- Forgiveness as protection for oneself
- Recognising envy and resentment
- Not repaying harm with harm

### Everyday Guidance

This figure counsels watchfulness over one's own heart. If someone has wronged you, address it but do not let it define you. It also advises caution around people whose behaviour is driven by resentment.

### Reflection

Which old hurt am I still feeding?

### In the Wider System

This is a principal figure (Odù Méjì). It is the first parent of 15 of the 240 compound figures (Omo Odù), and the second parent of another 15. Each of those inherits something of this figure's character and adds the influence of its partner.
