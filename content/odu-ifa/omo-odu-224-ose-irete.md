## Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ìrẹtẹ̀

### Overview

A late harvest. A long wait is followed by plenty, so patience pays off.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)
- **Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì** — perseverance despite setbacks, hidden victory (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọya)


### Guidance

Keep planting, and be ready for the harvest.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double) and the left column is Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì (single · single · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀ṣẹ́-Ìrẹtẹ̀ means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### From Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì

Ìrẹtẹ̀ teaches resilience in adversity, especially struggles others cannot see. It reassures that persistence — even quiet, unglamorous effort — leads to eventual triumph.

*Ìṣẹ́gun tí a kò fojú rí ni í dùn jù.*  
"The victory unseen by others is often the sweetest."

### Putting the two together

Practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline. At the same time, this figure reads as reassurance for those who feel stuck. Keep going, keep records of progress, and avoid measuring yourself against people whose circumstances differ. Results often arrive later than hoped but are lasting when earned.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
