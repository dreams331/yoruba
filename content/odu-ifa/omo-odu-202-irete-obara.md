## Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ọ̀bàrà

### Overview

Endurance without arrogance. Success after a long struggle can make a person proud, and pride can undo it.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì** — perseverance despite setbacks, hidden victory (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọya)
- **Ọ̀bàrà Méjì** — honour, boastfulness, the danger of pride, leadership (Ṣàngó / Ọ̀bàtálá)


### Guidance

Stay humble when the win finally comes.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì (single · single · double · single) and the left column is Ọ̀bàrà Méjì (single · double · double · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ọ̀bàrà means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì

Ìrẹtẹ̀ teaches resilience in adversity, especially struggles others cannot see. It reassures that persistence — even quiet, unglamorous effort — leads to eventual triumph.

*Ìṣẹ́gun tí a kò fojú rí ni í dùn jù.*  
"The victory unseen by others is often the sweetest."

### From Ọ̀bàrà Méjì

Ọ̀bàrà teaches that leadership and confidence are gifts, but boastfulness invites downfall. True honour is shown through humility and generosity, not empty declarations.

*Ìgbéraga ni í tẹ̀lé àṣejù ìṣọ́ra.*  
"Excess confidence is often followed by a fall."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as reassurance for those who feel stuck. Keep going, keep records of progress, and avoid measuring yourself against people whose circumstances differ. Results often arrive later than hoped but are lasting when earned. At the same time, read practically, this figure advises that a person should let results speak. Say less than you can do, deliver what you say, and share credit. Reputation built that way is stronger than one built on announcements.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
