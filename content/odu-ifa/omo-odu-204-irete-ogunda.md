## Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ògúndá

### Overview

Grit and craftsmanship. Hard work, repeated daily, builds a skill that no one can take away.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì** — perseverance despite setbacks, hidden victory (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Ọya)
- **Ògúndá Méjì** — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)


### Guidance

Practise every day, even when progress is slow.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì (single · single · double · single) and the left column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìrẹtẹ̀-Ògúndá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìrẹtẹ̀ Méjì

Ìrẹtẹ̀ teaches resilience in adversity, especially struggles others cannot see. It reassures that persistence — even quiet, unglamorous effort — leads to eventual triumph.

*Ìṣẹ́gun tí a kò fojú rí ni í dùn jù.*  
"The victory unseen by others is often the sweetest."

### From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

*Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n.*  
"A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

### Putting the two together

This figure reads as reassurance for those who feel stuck. Keep going, keep records of progress, and avoid measuring yourself against people whose circumstances differ. Results often arrive later than hoped but are lasting when earned. At the same time, this figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
