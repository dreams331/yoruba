## Ìwòrì-Ọ̀ṣẹ́

### Overview

Wisdom brings sweetness. Knowing yourself leads to contentment, and the figure encourages gratitude for what you already have.

### Governing Influences

- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì** — blessings, sweetness, abundance, gratitude (Ọ̀ṣun)


### Guidance

Appreciate your blessings, and share the joy that comes from a clear conscience.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double) and the left column is Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì (single · double · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìwòrì-Ọ̀ṣẹ́ means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### From Ọ̀ṣẹ́ Méjì

Ọ̀ṣẹ́ celebrates the sweetness of life earned through generosity, hospitality, and gratitude. It teaches that abundance flows to those who share freely and honour Ọ̀ṣun's spirit of grace.

*Ọwọ́ tí ó fúnni, ni Ọlọ́run í fi kún.*  
"The hand that gives is the one Olódùmarè fills."

### Putting the two together

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception. At the same time, practically, this figure suggests giving freely where one can, expressing thanks, and keeping a warm home and workplace. It also reminds a person that sweetness is not weakness, and that kindness takes discipline.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
