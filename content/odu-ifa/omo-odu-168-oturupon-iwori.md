## Òtúrúpọ̀n-Ìwòrì

### Overview

Know yourself before you treat yourself. Honest attention to your own habits explains much of what troubles you.

### Governing Influences

- **Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì** — illness and healing, secrets of the body, patience (Ọbàtálá / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
- **Ìwòrì Méjì** — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)


### Guidance

Keep a plain record of your health and routines, and act on what it shows.

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì (double · double · single · double) and the left column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Òtúrúpọ̀n-Ìwòrì means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Òtúrúpọ̀n Méjì

Òtúrúpọ̀n calls for patience, proper diagnosis before action, and respect for the body's natural healing processes. It also symbolises the wisdom that comes from surviving hardship.

*Àrùn tí a kò tọ́jú lọ́nà tí ó tọ́ kì í yá.*  
"An illness not treated in the right way does not heal."

### From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

*Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn.*  
"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

### Putting the two together

Read in daily life, this figure advises seeking the right help early, following treatment through, and resting enough. It also encourages care for people who are unwell, since recovery is helped by support. At the same time, tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
