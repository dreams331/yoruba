## Ìwòrì Méjì

> *"Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn."*
> *"If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."*

---

### Meaning

The Odu of self-knowledge — seeing what is hidden, including within oneself.

### Associated Òrìṣà

Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà

### Life Lesson

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

### Overview

Ìwòrì Méjì has a pattern of an open centre framed by closed marks, and its name is tied to the idea of looking inward and seeing. It is associated with insight, with discovering what lies beneath appearances, and with the discipline of examining one's own motives first.

### Core Themes

- Self-knowledge before judging others
- Hidden matters coming to light
- Careful observation and discernment
- Honesty in what one says about oneself

### Everyday Guidance

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception.

### Reflection

What am I refusing to look at in myself?
