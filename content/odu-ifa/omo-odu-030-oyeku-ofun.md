## Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Òfún

### Overview

A life reaches its natural completion with dignity. It honours old age, a clean conscience and a peaceful transition.

### Governing Influences

- **Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì** — endings, the ancestors, the unseen world, mortality (Ikú (Death) / Ọ̀sun)
- **Òfún Méjì** — purity, old age, completion, moral integrity (Ọbàtálá / Olódùmarè)


### Guidance

Live with integrity so that you may close each chapter in peace.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì (double · double · double · double) and the left column is Òfún Méjì (double · single · double · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Òfún means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú teaches respect for the ancestors, acceptance of mortality, and the understanding that every ending clears space for renewal. It warns against arrogance in the face of life's fragility.

*Òkú tó sun, kì í jí mọ́; ẹ̀mí tó lọ, kì í padà bí ara.*  
"The dead that sleeps does not wake again; the spirit that departs does not return in the same body."

### From Òfún Méjì

Òfún, closely tied to Ọbàtálá, teaches purity of character, patience, and the moral discipline required to age gracefully and finish life's journey with integrity intact.

*Funfun ni àrà; kò gba ìdọ̀tí láti dúró funfun.*  
"White is sacred; it takes discipline to remain unstained."

### Putting the two together

Read in daily life, this figure asks a person to honour those who came before, settle unfinished obligations, and stop treating limits as enemies. It cautions against pride about health, wealth or status, since all of them are borrowed for a time. At the same time, in daily terms, this figure advises keeping one's word, being slow to anger, and finishing what one starts. It is also a reminder that integrity is maintained through small choices made consistently.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
