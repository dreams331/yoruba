## Ogbè-Ògúndá

### Overview

Opportunity arrives with hard work attached. The way forward is open, but it must be cleared by effort, skill and perseverance.

### Governing Influences

* **Èjì Ogbè** — light, new beginnings, divine potential (Ọ̀rúnmìlà / Olódùmarè)
* **Ògúndá Méjì** — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)


### Guidance

Take up your tools and work steadily; the path will respond to determination.

<!-- READING_START -->

### How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Èjì Ogbè (single · single · single · single) and the left column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ogbè-Ògúndá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

### From Èjì Ogbè

You were not born by accident. Every person carries an ori (destiny) chosen before birth. Ogbè teaches that your task is to remember and fulfil your purpose, and to lead with clarity, honesty and generosity of spirit.

*Imọ́lẹ̀ kìí ṣe n̄ǹkan tí a fún ọ — imọ́lẹ̀ ni ọ jẹ́.*  
"Light is not something given to you — light is what you are."

### From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

*Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n.*  
"A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

### Putting the two together

When this figure is cast, tradition reads it as encouragement to act openly. Plan in the light, speak plainly, and avoid shortcuts that need to be hidden later. It also reminds a person that a good start still needs steady follow-through. At the same time, this figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.
