The Ọlọ́jọ́ Festival is one of the oldest and most spiritually significant festivals in Yorubaland, celebrated annually in Ile-Ife — the cradle of Yoruba civilization.

## History & Significance

Ọlọ́jọ́ means "owner of the day," referencing Ọ̀rànmíyàn, the legendary warrior-prince associated with the festival, and the day's connection to Ògún, the Òrìṣà of iron and war. The festival honors the founding of Ile-Ife and the reign of the Ọọ̀ni (the traditional ruler and spiritual head of the Yoruba people).

## What Happens

- Days of rites, processions, and traditional performances across Ile-Ife
- The Ọọ̀ni of Ifẹ̀ dons the sacred and ancient **Aare crown**, believed to carry immense spiritual power, in a rare public display
- Thousands of devotees, traditionalists, tourists, and dignitaries gather to witness the procession to the ancient Ọrànmíyàn staff/shrine

## Global Significance

As Ile-Ife is considered the spiritual homeland of all Yoruba people, Ọlọ́jọ́ draws visitors and diaspora devotees from across Nigeria, the Americas, and the Caribbean who trace their spiritual lineage to Ifẹ̀.
