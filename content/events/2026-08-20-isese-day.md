Ìṣẹ̀ṣe Day (also called African Traditional Religion Day) is observed on August 20 each year across Lagos, Ọ̀ṣun, Ọ̀yọ́, Ògùn, Ondo, and Ekiti states, celebrating indigenous Yoruba spirituality and heritage.

## Purpose

The day was instituted to give recognition and respect to adherents of Ìṣẹ̀ṣe (traditional Yoruba religious practice), who for generations faced marginalization compared to Christianity and Islam. It's now marked with public holidays in several states.

## Celebrations

- Colorful processions through major cities including Lagos
- Traditional worship and libations to the Òrìṣà
- Cultural displays of drumming, dance, and attire
- Participation from Ifá priests (Babaláwo), Ìyánífá, and traditional worshippers of Ṣàngó, Ọya, Ọ̀ṣun, and other deities

## Diaspora Connection

Communities in Brazil, Cuba, Trinidad, and the United States that practice Yoruba-derived religions (Candomblé, Santería/Lucumí, Vodou-adjacent traditions) often hold parallel observances.
