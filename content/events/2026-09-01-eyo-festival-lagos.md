The Eyo Festival, also called the Adamu Orisha Play, is a unique Lagos Island tradition featuring the "Eyo" masquerades — distinctive figures dressed in flowing white agbada-style robes and wide, colorful hats (aga), carrying a staff (opambata).

## History & Significance

Unlike most Yoruba festivals tied to fixed calendar dates, Eyo is staged on special occasions — historically to honor a departed Oba (king) or high chief of Lagos, or to mark significant civic events. Its irregular scheduling makes each outing highly anticipated by Lagosians.

## What Happens

- Different Eyo groups (representing various Lagos ruling houses) parade through the streets of Lagos Island in a symbolic procession
- Certain traditional rites and restrictions are observed on the day (e.g., no smoking, and women are traditionally advised to stay indoors during specific rites)
- The event culminates in a grand parade to the Lagos Iga Idunganran Palace

## Cultural Importance

Eyo represents the deep-rooted spiritual and monarchical traditions of the Lagos Yoruba (Aworis and Lagos indigenes), and is a proud symbol of the city's identity, often referenced as an inspiration for Brazil and the Caribbean's carnival masquerade traditions.

*Check local Lagos State Government and palace announcements for the next scheduled date, as Eyo outings are announced on a case-by-case basis rather than a fixed annual date.*
