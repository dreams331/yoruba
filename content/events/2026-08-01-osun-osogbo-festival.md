The Ọ̀ṣun Ọ̀ṣogbo Festival is one of the most significant traditional festivals in Yorubaland, held annually in the sacred Osun-Osogbo Grove — a UNESCO World Heritage Site along the banks of the Ọ̀ṣun River.

## History & Significance

The festival honors Ọ̀ṣun, the Òrìṣà of fertility, love, and the river that bears her name. According to tradition, the river goddess made a covenant with the founders of Osogbo, promising protection and prosperity in exchange for regular sacrifices and homage.

## What Happens

The two-week festival includes:
- Cleansing and purification rites at the palace
- Traditional wrestling and cultural displays
- A candlelight procession
- The crowning celebration where the **Ataoja of Osogbo** (traditional ruler) and the **Yeye Ọ̀ṣun** (chief priestess) lead thousands of devotees, tourists, and diaspora visitors to the grove for the climactic ceremony.

## Global Significance

The festival attracts visitors from across the Yoruba diaspora — Brazil, Cuba, the United States, and the Caribbean — many of whom practice Ọ̀ṣun devotion within Candomblé, Santería, and other Orisha-based traditions.
