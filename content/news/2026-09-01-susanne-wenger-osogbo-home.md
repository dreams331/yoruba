Susanne Wenger, who became a devotee of Ọ̀ṣun and a driving force behind the preservation of the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove (now a UNESCO World Heritage Site), left behind a home in Osogbo that has become a place of pilgrimage and cultural significance.

Wenger moved to Osogbo in the 1950s and was later initiated as an Ọ̀ṣun priestess. With local artists she founded the New Sacred Art movement, which restored shrines and built the sculptures that now stand in the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, recognised by UNESCO in 2005 as a World Heritage Site. The grove remains the centre of the annual Osun-Osogbo festival, which draws worshippers and visitors from around the world.
