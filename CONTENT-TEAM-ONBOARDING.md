## 📖 Part 1: The Business Case

### What Is Yoruba Heritage?

**Yoruba Heritage** is a free, publicly accessible digital platform dedicated to preserving, celebrating, and sharing the culture, history, language, and traditions of the Yoruba people — one of Africa's largest and most influential ethnic groups, with over **50 million people** in Nigeria alone and a global diaspora spanning Brazil, Cuba, the United Kingdom, the United States, and beyond.

### Why Does This Platform Exist?

Despite the richness of Yoruba civilisation — ancient kingdoms, world-class art, a complex philosophical tradition (IFA), and a globally influential diaspora — there is no single trusted, modern, English-first digital home for this knowledge.

Current problems we are solving:

| Problem | Our Solution |
|--------|--------------|
| Yoruba history is scattered across academic papers and outdated websites | We publish clear, readable articles for general audiences |
| Yoruba stories and oral traditions are being lost with each generation | We build a dedicated Stories section to preserve and share them |
| IFA wisdom is misunderstood or inaccessible outside specialist circles | We present IFA teachings in plain, respectful language |
| The diaspora feels disconnected from their roots | We have a dedicated Diaspora section covering 20+ countries |
| Young Yoruba people don't have a trusted resource in modern formats | We are mobile-first, modern, and designed for all ages |

### Who Is Our Audience?

1. **Yoruba heritage seekers** — people of Yoruba descent, at home and in the diaspora, who want to reconnect with their roots
2. **Students and researchers** — looking for reliable, well-written cultural and historical content
3. **Non-Yoruba people** — curious about West African culture, history, and spirituality
4. **Educators** — teachers and lecturers looking for accessible reference material
5. **Global diaspora communities** — in the UK, USA, Brazil, Canada, and 20+ other countries

### What Are Our Goals?

**Short-term (0–6 months):**
- Build a library of at least 50 high-quality articles across all categories
- Establish 20+ traditional stories in the archive
- Expand IFA wisdom content to at least 15 entries
- Grow the gallery with culturally significant images
- Reach our first 1,000 monthly visitors

**Medium-term (6–18 months):**
- Become the go-to reference site for Yoruba culture in English
- Build a newsletter community of 2,500+ subscribers
- Enable community contributions and comments
- Add Spanish and French content for the Latin American and Francophone diaspora
- Launch a Yoruba language version of the site

**Long-term (18+ months):**
- Partner with universities, museums, and cultural organisations
- Host live events and webinars
- Launch a charity/NGO arm to fund cultural preservation projects
- Build a marketplace for Yoruba artisans and creators
- Support a generation of Yoruba writers and storytellers

### Why This Matters

> *"A people without the knowledge of their past history, origin and culture is like a tree without roots."* — Marcus Garvey

Every article we publish, every story we record, and every tradition we explain is an act of cultural preservation. The Yoruba people have one of the richest civilisations in human history. This platform exists to make sure that legacy is never lost.

## 🖥️ Part 3: How to Add Content

### Accessing the CMS

1. Go to: **https://yorubaheritage.com/admin**
2. Click **"Login with Netlify Identity"**
3. Use the email and password you were invited with
4. You will land on the CMS dashboard

### Creating a New Article

1. In the CMS, click **"Articles"** in the left sidebar
2. Click **"New Article"** button
3. Fill in all fields:

| Field | What to Enter |
|-------|--------------|
| **Title** | Clear, descriptive headline (e.g. "Fela Kuti — Music as Resistance") |
| **Date** | Today's date |
| **Category** | Choose from the dropdown |
| **Featured Image** | Upload or paste a URL (use Unsplash for free images) |
| **Excerpt** | 1–2 sentence summary of the article (appears in card view) |
| **Read Time** | Estimate (e.g. "5 min", "8 min") |
| **Featured** | Only tick this for the very best articles |
| **Tags** | Add 3–5 relevant tags |
| **Body** | Write your full article here |

4. When ready, click **"Save"** (saves as draft) or **"Publish"** (makes it live)

> ⚠️ **Note:** All new content should be saved as **Draft** first. The project lead will review and publish.

### Writing in the CMS Body Editor

The body editor uses **Markdown**. Here are the basics:

```markdown
## This is a heading

This is a normal paragraph. Just type normally.

**This text is bold**

*This text is italic*

- This is a bullet point
- Another bullet point

1. This is a numbered list
2. Second item

> This is a quote or callout block

[Link text](https://example.com)
```

## ✍️ Part 4: Content Standards

### Writing Style

- **Voice:** Warm, authoritative, educational — like a knowledgeable friend, not an academic textbook
- **Audience:** Write for an educated general audience, not specialists
- **Language:** British English spelling (colour, honour, recognise)
- **Tone:** Proud and celebratory of Yoruba culture, never condescending
- **Accuracy:** Always verify facts. If unsure, say so or leave it out
- **Length:** Articles 600–1,500 words. Stories 400–800 words. IFA entries 300–600 words.

### Article Structure (Template)

```
## Introduction
(1–2 paragraphs setting the scene and what the article covers)

## [Main Section 1]
(3–4 paragraphs)

## [Main Section 2]
(3–4 paragraphs)

## [Main Section 3 — Optional]
(2–3 paragraphs)

## Conclusion / Legacy Today
(1–2 paragraphs connecting the topic to the present)
```

### What to Avoid

- ❌ Plagiarism — never copy from Wikipedia or other sites
- ❌ Unverified claims — if you can't source it, don't state it as fact
- ❌ Disrespectful language about Yoruba spiritual traditions
- ❌ Clickbait headlines
- ❌ Excessive use of AI-generated text without review and personalisation
- ❌ Publishing without saving as draft first

## 📌 Part 6: Quick Reference
