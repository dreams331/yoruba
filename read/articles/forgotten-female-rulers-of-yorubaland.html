Forgotten Female Rulers of Yorubaland

July 7, 2026 Yoruba Heritage Team

Introduction

When the history of Yorubaland is told, the stories of kings, warriors, and male rulers often take centre stage. Yet women also exercised political authority, led communities, influenced royal succession, commanded economic power, and shaped the course of Yoruba history. Some ruled as monarchs, while others held offices that gave them significant influence over governance, diplomacy, commerce, and military affairs.

Many of these women have received far less attention than their male counterparts, despite their lasting contributions. Their stories reveal that leadership in pre-colonial Yorubaland was more complex than is often assumed, with women occupying important positions in both political and social life.

Women in Yoruba Political Systems

Although most Yoruba kingdoms were headed by male monarchs, women were not excluded from political life.

Across Yorubaland, women served as:

* Senior chiefs. * Advisers to kings. * Market leaders. * Diplomats. * Religious leaders. * Military organisers. * Members of influential royal families.

Many kingdoms recognised the office of the Iyalode, the highest-ranking female chief, who represented the interests of women and often participated in major political decisions.

Queen Luwo Gbagida

Among the most remarkable figures in Yoruba tradition is Luwo Gbagida, who is remembered as the only woman traditionally recognised as having occupied the throne of the Ooni of Ile-Ife.

According to oral traditions, Luwo Gbagida ruled before the fifteenth century. She is credited with encouraging urban development and strengthening the influence of Ife. Some traditions also associate her reign with the expansion of paved courtyards and improvements to the royal city, although historians note that these accounts are difficult to verify through archaeology alone.

Despite the uncertainties surrounding some details of her reign, Luwo Gbagida remains a powerful symbol of female leadership in Yoruba history.

Moremi Ajasoro

Moremi Ajasoro is one of the best-known women in Yoruba history.

Although she was not a reigning monarch, her influence on the political survival of Ife was profound.

According to tradition, Moremi volunteered to infiltrate enemy forces that repeatedly attacked Ife. After learning their military secrets, she returned and shared the information with her people, enabling them to defeat their attackers.

Her story has become a lasting symbol of courage, sacrifice, and patriotism, and she is honoured through festivals and monuments in Ile-Ife.

The Iyalode

The title of Iyalode existed in many Yoruba kingdoms and carried significant political authority.

The Iyalode often:

* Represented women's interests before the king. * Participated in councils of chiefs. * Influenced public policy. * Resolved disputes involving women. * Organised commercial activities. * Supported community development.

Although not queens in the conventional sense, many Iyalodes exercised considerable influence over the political life of their kingdoms.

Madam Efunroye Tinubu

Madam Efunroye Tinubu lived during the nineteenth century, before the establishment of British colonial rule over Lagos and much of Yorubaland.

Although she was not a monarch, she became one of the most influential political and commercial figures of her time.

Operating from Badagry, Lagos, and later Abeokuta, she influenced royal succession, supported military campaigns, and built extensive trading networks.

Her leadership demonstrated that political influence in Yorubaland was not confined to the throne.

Female Regency in Yoruba Kingdoms

In several Yoruba kingdoms, women occasionally served as regents when a king died before a successor was installed or when the heir was too young to rule.

Regents helped:

* Maintain political stability. * Supervise palace administration. * Protect royal traditions. * Oversee succession.

Although these periods were usually temporary, they demonstrate that women could exercise executive authority when circumstances required.

Royal Women Behind the Throne

Many royal women exercised influence behind the scenes.

Queen mothers, senior wives, and royal princesses often:

* Advised monarchs. * Negotiated political alliances. * Influenced succession decisions. * Managed palace affairs. * Sponsored religious ceremonies. * Promoted peace between ruling families.

Their contributions rarely appeared in written records but were preserved through oral tradition.

Why Many Female Leaders Were Forgotten

Several factors contributed to the relative obscurity of many female leaders:

* The predominance of oral rather than written historical records. * Greater historical attention given to kings and military leaders. * Colonial-era interpretations that overlooked women's political roles. * The gradual loss of local historical traditions. * Limited documentation of female offices compared with royal institutions.

Modern historians are increasingly working to recover these overlooked histories by combining oral traditions, archaeology, and archival research.

Their Lasting Legacy

The legacy of Yoruba female leaders continues to inspire contemporary society.

Today, their stories encourage renewed appreciation for:

* Women's leadership. * Community service. * Political participation. * Cultural preservation. * Historical scholarship.

Festivals, monuments, museums, and academic research continue to highlight the contributions of women who helped shape Yoruba civilisation.

Conclusion

The history of Yorubaland was shaped not only by kings and warriors but also by remarkable women whose leadership left lasting marks on politics, commerce, diplomacy, and culture. Figures such as Luwo Gbagida, Moremi Ajasoro, and Madam Efunroye Tinubu demonstrate that women exercised influence in diverse ways, whether from the throne, the marketplace, the council chamber, or the royal palace.

While many of their achievements were overshadowed in later historical accounts, ongoing research continues to restore their rightful place in the story of Yoruba civilisation. Their lives remind us that the strength of Yorubaland has always depended on the contributions of both women and men, working together to build, protect, and sustain their communities.
