The Role of Community Assemblies in Decision Making

August 3, 2026 Yoruba Heritage Team

Introduction

Community assemblies were among the most important institutions of governance in traditional Yorubaland. Long before the establishment of modern local governments, elected councils, or legislative bodies, Yoruba communities gathered regularly to discuss matters affecting their towns and villages. These assemblies provided a forum where leaders consulted with the people, disputes were discussed, public policies were explained, and collective decisions were made.

Although traditional Yoruba kingdoms were led by monarchs, governance was rarely based on the ruler's authority alone. Community assemblies reflected the Yoruba belief that lasting decisions should be guided by consultation, wisdom, and the participation of respected members of society. In this way, they helped promote accountability, strengthen social cohesion, and preserve peace within the community.

  • - -

    • What Were Community Assemblies?

    Community assemblies were organised gatherings where members of a town or village met to discuss issues of public importance.

    Depending on the community, those present could include:

    * The traditional ruler. * Chiefs. * Councils of elders. * Family heads. * Religious leaders. * Representatives of community groups. * Skilled craftsmen and merchants.

    The exact structure varied from one kingdom to another, but the goal was the same: to make decisions that promoted the welfare of the community.

  • - -

    • Consultation Before Decisions

    One of the defining principles of Yoruba governance was consultation.

    Before important decisions were taken, leaders often sought advice on matters such as:

    * Security. * Trade. * Land use. * Festivals. * Public works. * Relations with neighbouring communities.

    This approach reflected the Yoruba saying that wisdom is strengthened when many thoughtful voices contribute to a decision.

  • - -

    • The Town Square as a Civic Space

    Many assemblies were held in open public spaces such as the town square or palace courtyard.

    These locations served several purposes:

    * Public discussion. * Royal announcements. * Conflict resolution. * Cultural ceremonies. * Community celebrations.

    Holding meetings in accessible places encouraged openness and reinforced the connection between leaders and the people.

  • - -

    • The Role of Elders

    Elders were highly respected because of their experience and knowledge of customary law.

    Their responsibilities included:

    * Offering advice. * Mediating disagreements. * Preserving oral history. * Interpreting customs. * Guiding younger leaders.

    While they were influential, they generally worked alongside other leaders rather than governing alone.

  • - -

    • Chiefs as Representatives

    Chiefs often represented different sections of the community.

    They brought concerns from:

    * Family compounds. * Town quarters. * Trade associations. * Military groups. * Religious institutions.

    By presenting the views of those they represented, chiefs helped ensure that a wide range of perspectives informed community decisions.

  • - -

    • Matters Discussed at Assemblies

    Community assemblies considered many aspects of public life.

    Common topics included:

    * Land allocation. * Farming schedules. * Market organisation. * Road maintenance. * Water resources. * Community security. * Festivals. * Dispute resolution. * Taxation and communal contributions.

    These discussions helped communities coordinate their activities and respond to changing circumstances.

  • - -

    • Building Consensus

    Rather than relying on simple majority voting, many Yoruba assemblies aimed to reach consensus.

    This meant:

    * Listening to different viewpoints. * Encouraging respectful debate. * Seeking compromise. * Finding solutions acceptable to the community.

    Consensus reduced divisions and strengthened commitment to collective decisions.

  • - -

    • Participation of Women

    Women also played important roles in community life.

    In many kingdoms, influential women leaders, including the Iyalode, represented the interests of women in public affairs. Women's associations could also organise community projects, contribute to discussions on trade and welfare, and advise traditional authorities on matters affecting families and markets.

    The extent of women's participation varied across different Yoruba kingdoms and historical periods.

  • - -

    • Community Labour and Development

    Assemblies often organised communal projects that benefited everyone.

    These included:

    * Repairing roads. * Maintaining markets. * Clearing pathways. * Building public structures. * Protecting water sources. * Supporting vulnerable members of the community.

    Collective labour reinforced the Yoruba value of shared responsibility.

  • - -

    • Accountability in Leadership

    Community assemblies also promoted accountability.

    Leaders were expected to:

    * Explain important decisions. * Listen to public concerns. * Respect customary law. * Govern in the interest of the community.

    While the influence of ordinary people differed from one kingdom to another, public consultation acted as an important check on leadership.

  • - -

    • Legacy in Modern Society

    Many traditions associated with community assemblies continue today.

    Town unions, traditional councils, village meetings, and community development associations still bring people together to discuss:

    * Local development. * Cultural preservation. * Conflict resolution. * Public welfare. * Community projects.

    These institutions reflect the enduring influence of indigenous Yoruba governance.

  • - -

Conclusion

Community assemblies were the foundation of participatory governance in traditional Yorubaland. By bringing together monarchs, chiefs, elders, family heads, and other respected members of society, they created opportunities for consultation, consensus, and collective responsibility.

Rather than concentrating power in a single individual, these assemblies encouraged dialogue, accountability, and cooperation. Their emphasis on shared decision-making helped maintain peace, resolve disputes, and promote the welfare of the community. Today, the legacy of these assemblies lives on in many traditional and civic institutions, demonstrating the lasting relevance of Yoruba principles of consultation, unity, and responsible leadership.
