Omo Odu

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Ọ̀wọ́nrín

Yoruba Heritage Team

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Ọ̀wọ́nrín

Overview

Change reaches the household of the ancestors. Old arrangements shift, and flexibility is the price of keeping peace in the family.

Governing Influences

  • Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì — endings, the ancestors, the unseen world, mortality (Ikú (Death) / Ọ̀sun)
  • Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì — instability, change, the trickster's lesson, humility (Èṣù / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)

Guidance

Adapt to new circumstances in the family and hold tradition with an open hand.

How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì (double · double · double · double) and the left column is Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì (double · double · single · single). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Ọ̀wọ́nrín means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

From Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú teaches respect for the ancestors, acceptance of mortality, and the understanding that every ending clears space for renewal. It warns against arrogance in the face of life's fragility.

Òkú tó sun, kì í jí mọ́; ẹ̀mí tó lọ, kì í padà bí ara. "The dead that sleeps does not wake again; the spirit that departs does not return in the same body."

From Ọ̀wọ́nrín Méjì

Ọ̀wọ́nrín teaches humility in success and resilience in reversal. It reminds us that fortunes change, so one must remain adaptable, avoid arrogance, and treat others well on the way up.

Ohun tó dúró jẹ́ẹ́, ni yíò yí padà. "What stands firm today may be overturned tomorrow."

Putting the two together

Read in daily life, this figure asks a person to honour those who came before, settle unfinished obligations, and stop treating limits as enemies. It cautions against pride about health, wealth or status, since all of them are borrowed for a time. At the same time, this figure suggests that a position held today can change tomorrow, so kindness and good relationships are better security than status. It also advises preparing for uncertainty rather than pretending it will not come.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.

---

Ọ̀yẹ̀kú-Ọ̀wọ́nrín is one of the 240 Omo Odù formed by combining two of the 16 principal Odù Méjì. Naming conventions for Omo Odù can vary between Ifá lineages; this entry uses a systematic right–left naming for clarity.
