Ìwòrì-Ògúndá

Yoruba Heritage Team

Ìwòrì-Ògúndá

Overview

Vision directs effort. It advises thinking carefully before acting so that hard work is spent on the right road.

Governing Influences

  • Ìwòrì Méjì — self-knowledge, hidden truth, inner vision (Ọ̀sanyìn / Ọ̀rúnmìlà)
  • Ògúndá Méjì — struggle, tools, perseverance, clearing the path (Ògún)

Guidance

Plan first, then labour with determination; insight makes effort effective.

How this figure is read

In Ifá, a figure is built from two columns of four marks, read from the right column to the left. Here the right column is Ìwòrì Méjì (double · single · single · double) and the left column is Ògúndá Méjì (single · single · single · double). Many diviners start with the right column and then bring in the left, though schools of Ifá differ in how they weigh the two. Reading Ìwòrì-Ògúndá means holding both lessons together and asking how they bear on the question at hand.

From Ìwòrì Méjì

Ìwòrì calls for introspection and honesty about one's own motives before judging others. It reveals secrets — both dangerous and beneficial — and teaches that true wisdom begins with self-awareness.

Bí a kò bá mọ ara ẹni, a kì í mọ ẹlòmíràn. "If one does not know oneself, one cannot truly know another."

From Ògúndá Méjì

Ògúndá honours the warrior spirit of Ògún — perseverance, the courage to clear obstacles, and the discipline of using one's tools and skills wisely rather than recklessly.

Ọ̀nà tí a kò gbóná, a kì í rìn án láìsí ọgbọ́n. "A path that has not been cleared cannot be walked without skill and effort."

Putting the two together

Tradition uses this figure to counsel reflection. Before blaming, ask what part is yours. Before trusting a story, look for what is missing from it. Insight gained this way can protect a person from deception, including self-deception. At the same time, this figure reads as encouragement to keep working at a difficult task and to prepare the right tools before starting. It also cautions that power without restraint becomes destructive, so strength should be directed by purpose.

Interpretations of individual Omo Odù differ between lineages and between diviners, so treat this page as a reading aid and not a ruling. A consultation with a trained Babaláwo or Ìyánífá will always be fuller than any written summary.

---

Ìwòrì-Ògúndá is one of the 240 Omo Odù formed by combining two of the 16 principal Odù Méjì. Naming conventions for Omo Odù can vary between Ifá lineages; this entry uses a systematic right–left naming for clarity.
